# Week 4 — Electronics, Microcontrollers, Sensors, and IoT

Week 4 focused extensively on electronics, microcontrollers, sensors, actuators, and IoT-based systems. The learning progressed from basic microcontroller concepts to integrating multiple components into interactive applications.

The week began with an introduction to Arduino Uno and the fundamentals of microcontroller-based systems. Basic concepts such as digital input and output, pins, and uploading programs to a board were explored. A simple LED project was used to understand the relationship between program instructions, digital signals, and physical output.

The next stage introduced sensors and actuators. Components such as PIR sensors, DHT11 temperature and humidity sensors, IR sensors, OLED displays, and joysticks were explored. The activities demonstrated how sensors provide input to a microcontroller, which can process that information and generate an appropriate output through actuators or displays.

The learning then progressed to ESP32-based systems. Components including an ultrasonic sensor, OLED display, and joystick were integrated with the ESP32 to understand how multiple devices can communicate with a single microcontroller. The ESP32 also introduced the concept of wireless connectivity and provided a stronger foundation for IoT applications.

The final part of the week involved developing interactive applications. A traffic controller simulation demonstrated how inputs, logical processing, and outputs could be combined into a working system. Another application involved an interactive digital pet using sensors and an OLED display, showing how sensor data could influence the state and behavior of a virtual system.

Week 4 provided a progression from **basic electronics → microcontrollers → sensors and actuators → ESP32 → IoT concepts → interactive applications**. It strengthened practical understanding of both hardware integration and software-controlled physical systems.
