# Week 2 — Problem Identification, Algorithms, and Application Development

Week 2 focused on moving from identifying problems to developing logical and practical solutions. The activities emphasized problem-solving, computational thinking, validation, creativity, and rapid application development.

The week began with identifying challenges within the retail industry and developing problem statements around potential areas of improvement. The use of structured problem-solving methods helped bring attention to workplace organization, efficiency, and continuous improvement.

Algorithms were then introduced as a foundation for logical thinking and programming. Activities explored how algorithms work, how different approaches can solve a problem, and how the choice of an approach can influence performance. This strengthened computational thinking and encouraged a more structured way of approaching problems.

Scratch provided an introduction to visual programming. Creating an interactive project demonstrated how programming logic can be represented visually while making the learning process more engaging. The activity also connected programming concepts with creativity and experimentation.

The second half of the week shifted toward validation and application development. Problem statements were reviewed and refined to ensure that they addressed meaningful needs. These ideas were then transformed into functional mobile applications using MIT App Inventor, providing hands-on experience with rapid application development and prototyping.

The major progression during Week 2 was from **problem identification → logical thinking → validation → prototyping → functional application development**. The week demonstrated how an initial idea can gradually become a working digital solution.
